# Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pairs a language model with an external
knowledge store so the model can ground its answers in retrieved evidence
instead of relying only on parameters learned at training time. A typical
pipeline has three stages: index a corpus of documents (chunk, embed, and
store them), retrieve the passages most relevant to a user's query, and
generate an answer conditioned on those passages plus the query.

RAG exists because parametric knowledge in a language model is frozen at
training time, expensive to update, and prone to hallucination on facts the
model never saw enough of. By injecting fresh, verifiable text into the
prompt at inference time, RAG lets a system answer questions about private
documents, recent events, or long-tail facts without retraining. It also
gives a system provenance: an answer can point back to the specific passage
it was derived from, which matters for trust and for auditing mistakes.

The quality of a RAG system is bounded by both halves of the pipeline. A
perfect retriever feeding a weak generator still produces answers that
misread the evidence; a strong generator fed irrelevant passages will either
hallucinate or refuse. This is why RAG systems are usually evaluated with
two separate metrics: retrieval quality (did we find the right passages) and
generation quality (did the model use them correctly). Improving one without
measuring the other is a common failure mode in early RAG projects.
