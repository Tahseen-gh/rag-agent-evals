# Hybrid Retrieval and Score Fusion

Hybrid retrieval combines a sparse method like BM25 with a dense embedding
method, then fuses their two rankings into one. The motivation is
complementary failure modes: BM25 is strong on exact lexical matches and
weak on paraphrase, while embeddings are strong on paraphrase and weaker on
rare exact tokens. Fusing them tends to outperform either method alone
across a broad query mix.

The simplest fusion approach is score-level: normalize each method's scores
to a comparable range (min-max normalization is common, since BM25 and
cosine similarity live on very different scales) and combine them with a
weighted sum, `score = (1 - alpha) * bm25_norm + alpha * embed_norm`. The
weight alpha controls how much the system trusts semantic versus lexical
signal, and is usually tuned on a held-out query set rather than guessed.

An alternative to a fixed hand-tuned alpha is a learned reranker: instead of
picking one weight for every query, train a small classifier (for example
logistic regression) on features like the BM25 score and the embedding
score, with relevance labels from real query-document pairs, and let it
learn the combination that best predicts relevance. This tends to
outperform a single global alpha because it can implicitly weight the two
signals differently depending on their magnitudes, at the cost of needing
labeled training data and a training step before it can be deployed.

Other fusion strategies exist, notably reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), which
combines rankings by position rather than by score and avoids the need to
normalize scores across methods at all.
