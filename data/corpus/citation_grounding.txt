# Citation and Answer Grounding

Citation grounding is the practice of having a RAG system attribute each
claim in its generated answer to the specific source passage it came from,
typically by having the model emit inline markers (like [1] or a document
id) alongside its answer that map back to the retrieved passages it was
given. This serves two purposes at once: it gives the end user a way to
verify a claim against the original source rather than trusting the model's
assertion outright, and it gives the system itself an implicit check —
requiring a citation for every claim discourages (though does not
guarantee against) statements that aren't actually supported by anything in
the retrieved context.

A citation is only as trustworthy as the mechanism that produces it.
Prompting a model to "cite your sources" without further verification is
weak: a model can hallucinate a plausible-looking citation to a real
document that doesn't actually contain the claim it's attached to. Stronger
implementations verify citations programmatically after generation — check
that the cited passage actually contains language supporting the claim
(via substring match, an entailment model, or an LLM-judge faithfulness
check) — and either flag or strip citations that don't hold up rather than
trusting the model's attribution at face value.

Citation grounding interacts directly with chunking and retrieval design:
if chunks are too large, a citation to "document 7" doesn't tell the reader
which of several claims in that document supports the answer, weakening the
citation's practical value even when it's technically correct. This is one
of several reasons retrieval and generation should be evaluated as a single
system rather than in isolation — a citation scheme that looks fine against
retrieval metrics alone can still fail to be useful to an actual reader.
