# Constitutional AI

Constitutional AI is a training approach, introduced by Anthropic, for
aligning a language model's behavior with a set of explicit principles (a
"constitution") using AI-generated feedback rather than relying solely on
large volumes of human-labeled preference data. It has two phases. In the
supervised phase, the model is prompted to critique and revise its own
responses against the constitution's principles, and is then fine-tuned on
the revised, improved responses. In the reinforcement learning phase, the
model itself (rather than a human) is used to compare pairs of responses
according to the constitution and produce preference labels, which train a
reward model that is then used to fine-tune the policy with reinforcement
learning — a process the authors termed RLAIF (reinforcement learning from
AI feedback) by analogy with RLHF.

The motivation is that human preference labeling is expensive, slow to
scale, and depends on the judgment and consistency of individual labelers,
whereas a written constitution makes the principles a model is meant to
follow explicit and auditable rather than implicit in a labeling
population's aggregate judgment. It also lets the desired behavior be
specified and iterated on directly — editing the constitution — rather than
only indirectly, by collecting new labeled examples that hopefully steer the
model in the intended direction.

A practical benefit reported for this approach is that it produces models
that are simultaneously more harmless and less evasive: models trained only
to avoid harm via aggressive human-labeled refusal often learn to refuse
overly broadly, including on benign requests that superficially resemble
harmful ones, whereas models trained against explicit principles can be
asked to explain their reasoning against those principles, which makes it
possible to distinguish a well-reasoned refusal from an overcautious one.
