# Evaluating RAG Answers with RAGAS-Style Metrics

Retrieval metrics like recall and NDCG tell you whether the right passages
were found, but not whether the generated answer actually used them
correctly. RAG-specific evaluation frameworks such as RAGAS decompose
answer quality into several distinct, separately measurable dimensions
rather than one opaque "is this good" score.

Faithfulness measures whether every claim in the generated answer is
actually supported by the retrieved context, typically by decomposing the
answer into individual statements and checking each one against the
context — this is the metric that catches hallucination even when the
answer sounds fluent and plausible.

Answer relevance measures whether the generated answer actually addresses
the question asked, independent of faithfulness; a fully faithful answer
that talks around the question rather than answering it should still score
poorly here.

Context precision and context recall evaluate the retrieval step within the
same framework: precision asks whether the retrieved passages that were
actually used are relevant (penalizing noisy context), recall asks whether
all the information needed to answer the question was present somewhere in
the retrieved context at all.

Because faithfulness and relevance require judging natural language rather
than comparing to a single fixed string, they are usually scored by another
LLM acting as a judge rather than by exact string match. This makes the
metric more flexible but also introduces judge variance, which is why
production RAG evals often pair an LLM-judge score with cheaper, more
deterministic checks (keyword or citation-presence checks) as a sanity
baseline.
