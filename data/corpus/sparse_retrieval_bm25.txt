# Sparse Retrieval and BM25

BM25 is a lexical scoring function that ranks documents by term overlap with
a query, extending simple TF-IDF with two corrections: it saturates the
contribution of very frequent terms (so a document repeating a word fifty
times isn't scored fifty times higher than one that says it once) and it
normalizes for document length (so short documents aren't unfairly favored
or penalized). The result is a ranking function that has stayed a strong
baseline for decades despite the rise of neural retrieval.

Because BM25 matches on tokens rather than meaning, it excels at exact-match
queries: product codes, function names, quoted phrases, rare technical
terms, or anything where the user's wording is likely to appear verbatim in
the relevant document. It also requires no training, no GPU, and no vector
index — just an inverted index over tokenized text — which makes it cheap
to stand up and fast to query even over millions of documents.

Its weakness is the mirror image of dense retrieval's strength: BM25 cannot
find a relevant passage that uses different words for the same concept. A
query for "how to cancel a subscription" will not match a document that only
says "terminate your plan" unless both phrasings happen to share enough
tokens. This complementary failure mode is exactly why hybrid retrieval
systems fuse BM25 with embeddings rather than picking one.
