# Rerankers

A reranker is a second-stage model that re-scores a small candidate set
returned by a fast first-stage retriever, trading extra compute per query
for higher precision at the top of the ranking. The two-stage pattern
exists because the models that are accurate enough to score a query against
every document individually (cross-encoders, or learned combiners of
retrieval features) are too slow to run over an entire corpus, while the
models fast enough to search millions of documents (BM25, dense bi-encoders)
are less accurate at fine-grained ranking.

A cross-encoder reranker takes the query and a candidate passage together as
a single input and outputs a relevance score, which lets it model
interactions between query and passage terms that a bi-encoder (which
embeds each independently) cannot capture. This makes cross-encoders
meaningfully more accurate at ranking a short list, at the cost of one
forward pass per candidate rather than one embedding lookup for the whole
corpus.

A lighter-weight alternative, useful when a full cross-encoder isn't
practical, is a learned linear or logistic-regression reranker over
retrieval-time features: the BM25 score, the embedding cosine similarity,
maybe positional or metadata features. This doesn't model query-passage
interactions as richly as a cross-encoder, but it is nearly free at
inference time and only requires a small labeled dataset of query-document
relevance pairs to train, which makes it a practical first step before
investing in a full cross-encoder.
