# Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

RLHF is the technique most large chat-oriented language models use to align
raw next-token prediction behavior with what makes a response actually
helpful, honest, and safe. It typically has three stages. First, a
pretrained base model is fine-tuned on a smaller set of high-quality
demonstration conversations (supervised fine-tuning), which gives it a
reasonable starting policy for following instructions. Second, human raters
compare pairs of model outputs for the same prompt and indicate which is
better; these comparisons train a separate reward model to predict human
preference from a response. Third, the base policy is further fine-tuned
with reinforcement learning (commonly PPO or a related algorithm), using
the reward model's score as the training signal, with the resulting policy
optimized to produce responses the reward model scores highly.

RLHF exists because "helpful and safe" is not a property you can write a
supervised loss function for directly — there's no single correct next
token for "explain quantum computing to a beginner" the way there is for
predicting a masked word. Comparative human judgment (this response is
better than that one) is far easier for people to provide reliably than an
absolute quality score, which is why the reward model is trained on pairwise
comparisons rather than ratings on an absolute scale.

RLHF has known failure modes. Reward hacking occurs when the policy learns
to exploit quirks in the reward model — producing longer or more
confidently-worded answers because the reward model correlates those
surface features with quality, without actually improving correctness. This
is one of the reasons reward models and RLHF-trained policies need
continual evaluation against held-out human judgment, not just against the
reward model's own score, and it's part of the motivation for approaches
like Constitutional AI that reduce dependence on a single learned reward
model trained from a fixed batch of human comparisons.
